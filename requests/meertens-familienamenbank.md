# Draft: request to Meertens Instituut / CBG for the Nederlandse Familienamenbank

**Not sent.** Writing to a third party on David's behalf needs his say-so. This is
ready to send from his own address, or to adapt.

**To:** info@meertens.knaw.nl (and/or info@cbg.nl)
**Subject:** Permission to reuse Nederlandse Familienamenbank data in an open, CC0 genealogy index

---

Dear colleagues,

I maintain **Record Atlas** (https://daviddef.github.io/TheMap/), a free, non-commercial,
open-source index of where genealogical records are held — which archive holds which ground,
for which years, and what it costs to look. It carries no advertising beyond a single footer
unit, holds no personal data, and its own datasets are published CC0.

Part of it is a surname dataset: every spelling a surname takes and the countries it is
attested in, built so that a researcher holding a name and nothing else knows which country
to start in. It currently draws on four national registers — the Polish PESEL list, the US
Census 2010 surname file, Spain's INE census frequencies and Norway's SSB table — for a total
of about 480,000 surnames.

**The Netherlands is a gap I would rather ask about than work around.** CBS publishes no
surname frequencies, and the Nederlandse Familienamenbank is plainly the authoritative Dutch
source. I have not scraped it and do not intend to.

I would be grateful to know:

1. Whether any extract of the familienamenbank — even name and frequency alone, without the
   etymological and distribution material — may be reused in an openly licensed dataset, and
   under what attribution and licence conditions.
2. If not CC0, whether a CC BY extract would be acceptable; I would then hold it separately
   from the CC0 file so the attribution requirement travels correctly rather than being lost
   in a merge.
3. Whether there is an existing route for this I have missed.

I am happy to credit the Meertens Instituut and the CBG prominently, to link to the
familienamenbank from every Dutch surname, and to exclude anything you would prefer not to see
redistributed.

With thanks for the work itself — it is the best of its kind in Europe.

Yours sincerely,
David Defranceski
