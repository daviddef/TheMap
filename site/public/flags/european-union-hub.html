Flags of the European Union

Every other hub in this atlas traces literal sovereignty — a border dissolving on a map because the same crown, empire, or government controlled both sides of it. The European Union is a fundamentally different kind of thing, and this hub treats it that way: it is a voluntary institutional membership that 27 fully self-governing countries have opted into, not a government that rules them. No EU member state stopped being sovereign the day it joined. Each still runs its own elections, courts, military, and (for most) currency policy; what changes is that the member pools specific, treaty-defined powers — a shared market, common trade rules, freedom of movement, and for 20 of the 27, a shared currency — through institutions the member states themselves created, sit on, and can in principle leave, as the United Kingdom did in 2020.

The institution's lineage runs through four treaties. The 1951 Treaty of Paris created the European Coal and Steel Community among six founding members — Belgium, France, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, and West Germany — pooling coal and steel production as a practical, low-drama way to make another European war between France and Germany "not merely unthinkable, but materially impossible." The 1957 Treaty of Rome broadened that into the European Economic Community, a genuine common market effective 1958. The 1993 Maastricht Treaty formally created "the European Union," layering political and monetary cooperation (the euro, introduced for transactions in 1999 and cash in 2002) on top of the existing economic community. Six enlargement waves since then brought membership from 6 countries to a peak of 28, before the UK's 2020 departure brought it back to 27.

How to read this hub. Because the EU doesn't govern its members the way, say, the Habsburgs governed Austria-Hungary, this page skips the atlas's usual real-map, era-slider visualization that shows conquered or inherited territory changing hands. A genuinely accurate EU visualization — showing voluntary accession and pooled, revocable authority rather than dissolving borders — is a distinct project deferred atlas-wide to a later phase, not something to fake here with the wrong mechanic. What this page does instead: real accession history below, and for the 24 of 27 members that already have their own dedicated flag-timeline page elsewhere in this atlas, a direct link to it.

About this hub

This hub is a cross-cutting index, not a queue of placeholders: 24 of the 27 current member states already have their own dedicated flag-timeline page built in earlier phases of this atlas, and their cards above link straight to them. Only Cyprus, France, and Malta are still publishing-next. That's the opposite of a typical hub here, where every country card is a placeholder because the region hasn't been built out yet.

The EU is institutional membership, not shared sovereignty. Unlike, say, the Habsburg lands or Yugoslavia hubs, no single map of "who controlled this territory" would be accurate here — every member state has controlled its own territory continuously since well before joining. What actually changed at each accession date is which treaty-bound club a fully independent government chose to join, and how much decision-making it agreed to pool with the others in exchange for the single market, freedom of movement, and (for most members) the euro. A visualization built for that voluntary, revocable, multi-layered kind of membership — rather than the dissolve-on-shared-ruler map used elsewhere in this atlas — is planned as its own later-phase project, not attempted here with the wrong tool.
