Flags of San Marino

San Marino calls itself the oldest surviving sovereign state and the oldest constitutional republic in the world — a claim resting on a founding tradition that reaches back to 301 CE, a body of statutes from 1600 still partly in force, and an unbroken record of never once being conquered, through the Renaissance, Napoleon, Italian unification, and two World Wars.

Only three eras mark this page, because San Marino's political system barely changed across seventeen centuries of self-government; its flag's white-over-light-blue bicolor and three-towers coat of arms simply became more formally fixed over time, not fundamentally different.

Scope of this page: the Republic of San Marino on Mount Titano, traditionally c. 301 CE to present, three phases. San Marino has governed itself continuously throughout its history — no internal partition, no foreign annexation ever completed. Part of a larger atlas — see the map and linked pages at the bottom.

One mountain, never conquered

San Marino's own real border around Mount Titano, traced whole — the world's smallest and one of its oldest surviving republics has governed the same small patch of the Apennines continuously since its traditional 301 CE founding. 1862 – present

Republic of San Marino (modern arms)

Republic, treaty-recognizedNo single ruler documented (Captains Regent)Roman Catholic (traditional, no state church)
Migration & conquest:Occupied by: Germany (Sept. 1944)Immigration: Italian refugees fleeing WWII bombing (1944)

Who & why: as the Kingdom of Italy forms around it, San Marino secures its continued independence through a friendship treaty with Italy in 1862 (renewed and updated since), the same year it fixes the current form of its state arms — the three towers of Guaita, Cesta, and Montale atop Mount Titano — on the flag. The republic stays neutral through the First World War, is briefly occupied by retreating German forces for about two weeks in September 1944 despite its declared neutrality, and continues today as a functioning parliamentary republic and a founding-era relic that outlasted every empire that ever bordered it.

What it represents: white for the snow on Mount Titano's peaks, light blue for the sky above it; the three towers, crowned and wreathed with laurel and oak beneath the word LIBERTAS, mark San Marino's central civic identity — liberty defended by its own mountain fortress.

1600 – 1862

Republic under the 1600 Statutes

Self-governing republicNo single ruler documented (Captains Regent)Roman Catholic (traditional, no state church)

Who & why: in 1600, San Marino codifies its Leges Statutae Republicae Sancti Marini, a written constitution — among the oldest still partly in force anywhere in the world — formalizing a self-governing republic ruled by two Captains Regent elected every six months. The republic weathers the Renaissance-era ambitions of neighboring powers, and when Napoleon's armies sweep through Italy in the 1790s, he personally declines to annex San Marino, reportedly offering to expand its territory instead; San Marino, wary of the offer's strings, politely refuses.

What it represents: the white-over-light-blue bicolor is already in traditional use during this era, representing the snow-capped peak of Mount Titano beneath the open sky, though the state coat of arms is not yet fixed to a single standard design on the flag itself.

c. 301 – 1600

Traditional founding to the early commune

Self-governing republicMarinus (traditional founder)Roman Catholic (traditional, founded as a Christian refuge)
Migration & conquest:Immigration: Marinus & Christian refugees (traditional, 301 CE)

Who & why: tradition holds that Marinus, a Christian stonemason from Rab fleeing the Diocletianic persecutions, founds a small community of fellow Christians on Mount Titano in 301 CE — a date widely repeated and honored by San Marino itself (its calendar year famously runs 1,700 years ahead in local reckoning), but not independently documented. The earliest solid documentary evidence of a self-governing community on the mountain is a charter dated 885 CE; by the high Middle Ages a recognizable commune with its own statutes and elected officials is firmly attested, laying the groundwork for the formal 1600 constitution.

What it represents: no flag is documented from the earliest, legendary period of this span. The white-and-blue color scheme is traditionally associated with San Marino from deep antiquity, but its first firm attestation as a flag comes centuries later; it is shown here across this whole era as the same honest, undated tradition rather than a documented early banner.
