Flags of the Republic of the Congo

Congo-Brazzaville takes its name from the city Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza founded on the Congo River's north bank in 1880 — the administrative capital that grew into the seat of all French Equatorial Africa. Its own path runs from the Teke Kingdom that ceded land to Brazza's French mission, through eight decades as a French territory, a brief and genuinely radical run as a Marxist-Leninist one-party republic, and back to multiparty rule under the pan-African tricolor it first raised at independence.

It is easy to confuse with its much larger neighbor across the river, the Democratic Republic of the Congo — the two capitals, Brazzaville and Kinshasa, are the closest pair of national capitals in the world after Rome and Vatican City — but their colonial paths (French versus Belgian) and post-independence histories diverge sharply.

Scope of this page: the lands of the present-day Republic of the Congo, from the pre-colonial Teke Kingdom through French Equatorial Africa to the modern republic. Republic of the Congo (multiparty republic)

Republic, multipartyPascal LissoubaSecular (no state religion)
Migration & conquest:Emigration: war refugees (1997–2003)Immigration: refugees from Zaire/DRC wars (1997–)

Who & why: a 1991 National Conference strips the ruling Congolese Labour Party of its constitutional monopoly and restores the pre-1970 flag alongside multiparty elections. The transition is not smooth — a 1997 civil war returns Denis Sassou Nguesso to power by force, and he has governed continuously (aside from a 1992–97 interlude) ever since — but the flag itself has not changed since 1991.

What it represents: the same green-yellow-red diagonal tricolor first adopted at independence in 1960, restored as the definitive national symbol after the Marxist-Leninist interlude ended.

1970 – 1991

People's Republic of the Congo

Republic, one-party (Marxist-Leninist)Marien NgouabiState atheism (Marxist-Leninist)
Migration & conquest:Immigration: Angolan & southern African liberation-movement exiles (1975–)

Who & why: after a series of coups following independence, Marien Ngouabi declares the People's Republic of the Congo in December 1969, formalized in January 1970 — sub-Saharan Africa's first officially Marxist-Leninist state, aligned with the Soviet bloc under the sole ruling Congolese Labour Party (PCT). The system persists through Ngouabi's 1977 assassination and successive PCT leaders until the 1991 National Conference ends one-party rule.

What it represents: a red flag with a gold star, hammer and hoe within palm branches in the canton (Wikipedia, dated 1970 to 1991), replacing the tricolour; Flags of the World says the replacement was based on the Soviet flag and dates the abolition of the old flag to 30 December 1969.

Republic of the Congo (independence)

Republic, independentFulbert YoulouSecular (no state religion)

Who & why: the territory becomes an autonomous republic within the French Community in 1958 and adopts this flag in September 1959, ahead of full independence on 15 August 1960 under Fulbert Youlou, the country's first president. Brazzaville, already the administrative seat of all French Equatorial Africa, becomes the new republic's capital.

What it represents: the green-yellow-red diagonal tricolour (Wikipedia: adopted 15 September 1959; Flags of the World: 18 August 1959), the only Pan-Africanist flag with a diagonal according to Wikipedia. The sources read do not credit the colours to Ethiopia and Ghana.

1880 – 1959

French Congo / French Equatorial Africa

ColonyPierre Savorgnan de BrazzaRoman Catholic (favored, not established)
Migration & conquest:Immigration: forced labor for Congo-Ocean Railway (1921–34)

Who & why: Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza negotiates a treaty with Teke ruler Makoko in 1880, founding the post that becomes Brazzaville and establishing French claims north of the Congo River, formalized as French Congo in 1891. From 1910 the territory anchors French Equatorial Africa, a federation of four colonies (alongside Gabon, Ubangi-Shari, and Chad) governed from Brazzaville — making this small territory the administrative heart of French holdings across the whole of central Africa.

What it represents: Wikipedia says the French authorities reportedly forbade the colony its own flag; the sources read do not describe the colonial flag or mention the Tricolour for this period, and the 1880 start date is not covered.

c. 1400 – 1880

Teke Kingdom (Kingdom of Anzico) and neighboring polities

Pre-colonial kingdomNo single ruler documentedTraditional/ancestral religion
Ruling house:Makoko (Teke royal title)Maloango (Loango royal title)

Who & why: the Teke (Bateke) Kingdom, also recorded by early European visitors as Anzico, controls the plateau north of the Congo River from at least the 15th century, trading ivory and copper into networks that reach the Kingdom of Kongo to the south. It is the Teke ruler Makoko who signs the 1880 treaty with Brazza that becomes the basis for French claims to the territory.

What it represents: no securely documented flag or banner survives for the Teke Kingdom or the smaller polities along this stretch of the river; shown here honestly blank rather than invented.
